AC Leak Water Damage Help in Santa Maria

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ACNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Bring In Two ProsCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • Poorly connected drain piping, which lets water out before it reaches the outlet.
  • The safety float switch being absent or broken, so the unit kept running.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Mold Near the Return

Mold may appear in 24 to 48 hours on damp materials by the air handler, where the return air stream can carry its spores through the ductwork to other rooms.

Floors Outside the Closet

Water runs out under the closet door and gets beneath carpet pad, laminate or hardwood. Swollen laminate does not go back, and wet hardwood left alone will cup.

Ceilings Under the Unit

Overflow from a unit in the attic lands on the ceiling drywall. First comes a stain, next a sag, and after days of soaking a piece may drop into the bedroom or hall underneath.

Baseboards and Door Trim

The bottom of the door jamb and the baseboard beside it take on water early. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • Length of the leak, counted from the first drip to the day the system was stopped.
  • Where the unit sits: attic, closet or garage.
  • Whether the wood platform under a closet unit has to be rebuilt.
  • Whether mold has started and needs containment and removal.
