A dry house is not yet a finished house. Even with normal moisture readings, you are left with opened walls, flooring that is gone and exposed subfloor. Water damage repair is the rebuild. In Santa Maria, we put back the materials that were lost and match them to the originals, turning a work zone into a normal room again.

No two repair jobs are the same size. A minor loss can need just a couple of feet of wallboard plus baseboard and a coat of paint. Large jobs can include insulation, whole drywall sheets, subfloor, laminate or hardwood, cabinet boxes, doors and trim. Where we can, replacements are like for like, and if no exact match exists you will hear it plainly and make the choice.

We do not close anything in while it is still damp. A moisture meter has to show normal levels in the subfloor and framing before new material is installed. How to Prevent Water Damage Repair Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Completed-repair photos and the scope document belong in your home records.
  • Take pictures of damaged materials prior to removal, since the insurer may want them later.
  • Fresh caulk every year around showers, tubs and sinks stops water reaching the wall cavity.
  • Laundry rooms, bathrooms and kitchens should be rebuilt with moisture resistant wallboard and proper backer board.
  • Ventilate with the bathroom exhaust fan while showering and afterward to protect new drywall.
  • Do not paint or patch over a water stain until the source is fixed.

How to Tell You Need Water Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Trim around doors and along the floor is bowed and will not lie flat.
  • During drying, sections of wallboard came out and the framing is still exposed.
  • Wood floor boards stayed raised, cupped or crowned even once the moisture was gone.
  • Since the water damage, certain doors drag on the jamb or miss the latch.
  • Ceiling drywall is sagging, cracked or crumbling after a leak from above.
  • Tiles sound hollow, have come loose or show cracked grout since water got beneath them.
  • The subfloor gives or feels spongy underfoot where water once stood.
